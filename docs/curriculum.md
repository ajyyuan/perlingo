# Perlingo curriculum specification

Status: **course specification v1, 2026-10-18; full content draft authored locally**. All 16 inventories and 384 new examples per language are present, with production, contrast and transfer practice. See [actual counts and verification limits](course-authoring.md). Evidence coverage, complete component analysis, release review and the proposed mastery/progression system are not complete. C4 AI chat remains separate.

## Scope and endpoint

Proposed default: an **A1-oriented core (U01–U08)** followed by an **A2-oriented extension (U09–U16)** for adult beginners learning Spanish, French, and German in parallel. These are Perlingo design bands, not official assignments of every grammar point to a CEFR level. The full first-course scope is these 16 units; B1 and beyond, specialist vocabulary, and an eventual 10,000-item library are outside this release.

The core should support introductions, basic needs, routines, descriptions, shopping, directions, and simple plans. The extension should support short accounts of past events, comparisons, requests, explanations, and connected everyday messages. CEFR describes communicative abilities, rather than prescribing our unit order or a universal word count. Our endpoints draw on that distinction; our sequence and numerical targets below are product proposals. [Council of Europe global scale](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-1-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-global-scale), [descriptor framework](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors).

Perlingo currently teaches through comparison, pronunciation playback, and exact typed recall. It does **not** assess listening comprehension, spontaneous speech, interactive conversation, or free writing. Completing this curriculum can demonstrate mastery of its taught material, not certify A1/A2 proficiency. A future claim of broader proficiency needs separate tasks and assessments in those skills.

## Course organization

Keep **Nouns, Verbs, Grammar, Sentences** as the four main categories. A unit is a communicative context spanning those categories; a grammar topic is an exercise target. Neither replaces the other. Adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, determiners, prepositions, numbers, and fixed expressions are taught inside grammar/sentence material while retaining their real lexical part of speech. Do not label an adjective as a noun just to fit the interface.

Units run in the order below. Each assumes previous units; the prerequisite column names especially important dependencies. A unit contains smaller lessons, not one enormous session. Parallel learning shares intentions and situations, not identical syntax, word counts, or grammar difficulty. When an equivalent expression requires later grammar in one language, introduce it as an explicitly explained fixed expression or provide a simpler natural equivalent; defer productive manipulation until its prerequisite is taught.

Not every cross-language row represents a one-to-one grammatical equivalent. In particular, German event/background meaning is not a translation exercise in French/Spanish aspect morphology. Recognizing a polite formula does not mean mastering the conditional or subjunctive system.

Beyond this course: systematic subjunctive use, counterfactual conditionals, advanced passive/reported speech, literary tense production, advanced case/relative constructions, and specialist vocabulary. Common formulas containing these may appear with a bounded explanation; they do not count as productive mastery of the wider construction.

## Vocabulary inventory and counting

Proposed planning budgets **per language**, cumulative: **800 lexical sense-items by U08; 1,600 by U16**. Budget roughly 100 new items per unit, adjusted for actual usefulness. These are capacity targets, not CEFR requirements or a promise that the exact same 1,600 English prompts translate cleanly into all languages.

| Lexical type | Core budget | Total through extension |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Noun senses | 300 | 600 |
| Verb senses | 120 | 240 |
| Adjective senses | 100 | 200 |
| Adverb senses | 60 | 120 |
| Function words, numbers, and other grammatical items | 120 | 240 |
| Fixed expressions | 100 | 200 |
| Total | 800 | 1,600 |

A lexical sense-item is one lemma+meaning or one conventional multiword expression in a language. Inflected forms, grammatical persons, noun plurals, spelling variants, and repeated occurrences do not inflate the count. Different useful meanings of a polysemous lemma are separate records. Some items align to a shared concept; others need language-specific records. A sentence is not automatically another vocabulary item. Grammar exercises and sentence examples have separate coverage counts.

Select by communicative need first, then within each language by frequency and dispersion across relevant contexts. A high-frequency function word may matter more than the next concrete noun. For every candidate, record sense, domain/unit, productive versus recognition goal, variety/register, frequency source/corpus version and rank if actually measured, lexical evidence, and reviewer. No invented frequency ranks. Goethe’s A1/A2 wordlists are useful German reference inventories, not substitutes for pedagogical sequencing or translation verification. [A1 inventory](https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/de/A1_SD1_Wortliste_02.pdf), [A2 inventory](https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/en/Goethe-Zertifikat_A2_Wortliste.pdf).

The exact inventories are now authored for all units. The type budgets above remain planning allocations rather than exact quotas: the draft contains more nouns/adjectives and useful expressions than the original allocation. Actual deduplicated counts and counting exclusions are documented in [course authoring](course-authoring.md). Frequency and dispersion ranking has not been performed. Non-noun/verb lexical practice uses the sentence quota while retaining its lexical type.

## Sentence and exercise authoring contract

For each language-specific target cluster, require at least **8 reviewed contextual examples** across at least two situations. Include at least four productive prompts, two comprehension/contrast tasks, and two held-out transfer prompts with new combinations. Reuse can reinforce another objective but count an example toward only one primary target. With 3 clusters × 16 units, the minimum is **384 examples per language, 1,152 language versions**; the core minimum is 192 per language. This is an authoring floor, not all exercises needed for fluent use.

Most lesson examples should introduce one target construction and use previously taught vocabulary. Introduce an indispensable new word first or explicitly gloss it; do not silently test unknown vocabulary. Vary subjects, number, polarity, and context where relevant, rather than producing eight copies with a noun swapped. Author idiomatic sentences first, then align their intent across languages. Store original text, target objective, word/chunk boundaries, contextual meaning, form features, acceptable variants, and evidence. Corpus examples still need sense, level, license, and naturalness review. Generated examples are candidates only.

A grammar exercise isolates a form/contrast. A sentence exercise integrates already introduced targets into communication. Keep grammatical function words inside their useful phrase; the sidebar can explain components. A compact popover answers meaning here, the sidebar explains form. No English word-for-word segmentation requirement.

Accepted-answer policy must precede release of a unit: specify alternatives for optional subject pronouns, register, regional vocabulary, equivalent word order, and contractions. Reject changes that alter the intended tense, referent, or meaning. The app accepts the taught string plus explicitly authored alternative strings, using its existing normalization. Two-choice comprehension and held-out-from-learning transfer modes are implemented. Open-ended semantic evaluation is **not implemented**. Do not describe that exact-match behavior as evaluating all valid translations.

## Varieties, conjugation, and review

Proposed initial production tracks: Spain-oriented Spanish, France-oriented French, and Germany-oriented Standard German, matching current locale choices. Label those choices and source regional alternatives; they are not claims of universal usage. Existing Spanish vos and ustedes forms remain accessible reference variants; do not mix address systems inside an unlabelled exercise. Regional specialists must check naturalness and accepted variants. A later track can change production targets without overwriting already learned records.

Display all relevant person/number forms for the taught tense, with auxiliary, separability, reflexive/clitic behavior, irregularity, and agreement notes where needed. Reference tables may show more than a lesson tests; table presence is not curriculum coverage. Keep recognition of a form separate from productive practice. Do not expand every verb across every possible tense before its unit needs it.

Local authoring workflow: candidate → source comparison → contextual checks → local practice and correction. The user has accepted this method; independent linguistic review is additional assurance and is not required before continuing authoring. The existing publication admission workflow remains candidate → source comparison → independent linguistic review → reuse review → approved pack, pending a separately authorized release-policy change. Review language versions separately; one correct Spanish version does not approve French/German. Record reviewer/date and unresolved ambiguities. Author review, source comparison, and user approval of code are not independent linguistic approval. Keep release validation strict. Corrections preserve stable progress IDs unless the taught sense changes materially; content versioning and migration rules must then be designed before changing progress semantics.

## Progress and completion requirements (proposed, not implemented)

Keep the existing one-perfect-round session rule. A session pass is not a unit pass. Proposed unit readiness requires all mandatory objectives attempted, at least 90% unassisted first-attempt accuracy on a balanced 20-prompt checkpoint per language, no target cluster below 75%, and at least 80% on a delayed 10-prompt check after seven days. Include new combinations; do not test only memorized training sentences. These thresholds are starting product decisions to calibrate with learner trials, not established CEFR cut scores.

Track outcomes separately per language and objective. Revisit weak prerequisites without forcing every language to repeat mastered material. Learners may preview later units; future progression should recommend readiness rather than falsely declare proficiency. Help/reveals must warn beforehand, mark the response assisted, and exclude it from perfect-round/mastery credit. No progression engine, checkpoint mode, objective scheduling, or mastery dashboard is introduced by this specification.

## Implementation and remaining acceptance work

All 16 unit inventories and the 4/2/2 example mix are authored and playable. Existing seed IDs/evidence are retained. The local course includes unit selection, explicit answer variants, contrast and transfer tasks, reference-form selection and coverage auditing.

Remaining verification work includes full source/sense and sentence comparisons, detailed component notes, concrete situation metadata (current examples retain generic situation labels), comprehensive accepted-answer policies and reuse review. The specification’s reviewed-examples/two-situations requirement is not satisfied merely by the numeric example count. Proposed prerequisite recommendations, objective scheduling, checkpoints and delayed assessment remain separate implementation work. These limitations do not block authorized local authoring or preview, and must not be described as implemented.

Unit release still requires reviewed inventories/examples, correct chunks/forms, appropriate accepted answers, source/reuse metadata and strict pack admission. No CEFR proficiency guarantee is implied.
