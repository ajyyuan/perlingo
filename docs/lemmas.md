### Learning and tests

Day tests and context reviews have a **Restart test** text control in the test footer and completion screen. It immediately starts a fresh shuffled 250-question attempt, clearing that test’s score, repairs, feedback and draft. Set progress, other tests and word history stay saved. The new attempt saves through the existing local/account progress path and resumes after reload.

- **Batch (10):** read ten English meanings and Spanish headwords, then type the Spanish headwords from English prompts in shuffled order.
- **Set (50):** after five batch tests, choose English meanings for all fifty Spanish words. Four distinct glosses come from the set, falling back to the deck if needed. Choice order stays stable during feedback and reload.
- **Day (250):** typed Spanish recall covers every card in the selected day, not a sample. Your days permits starting early; there is no daily lock.
- **Context review:** all six days have 250 questions each, one per lemma/POS card. Read the English translation, then complete one missing Spanish word. The required surface form can differ from the headword (somos for ser, hay for haber, primer for primero). Surrounding Spanish words show meanings on hover/focus and pronounce on click. The hidden word is absent from tooltips and pronunciation attributes until submission. Feedback restores the sentence and explains the form; supplying the headword when an inflected form is required produces a form-error message. Days 2–6 show the previous day’s review above the day’s sets. Every day also has its own review below its day test, so Day 6 remains reviewable without a seventh-day screen. Reviews are optional to start and resumable; they use the same mandatory repairs and perfect full pass as the other tests.

### Passing, mistakes and scores

Every implemented test uses the same rules. Finish the entire shuffled full pass even after a mistake. If it has misses, repair each with an unaided correct answer. A short repair queue includes up to three already encountered words so a lone miss is not simply repeated immediately; errors on those words also require repair. The last question is not immediately repeated at the start of repair when other questions exist. Finishing repair starts a new shuffled full pass, not stage completion. A stage clears only after a full pass with no errors. A perfect first pass clears directly. There are no mistake limits, mid-pass restarts, timers or uninterrupted-session requirements.

Show answer counts as a miss. After three incorrect attempts on a word, feedback offers its meaning, pattern and tip for relearning; this never changes the recorded result. The first full pass's score stays frozen through repairs and later full passes. The displayed Pass number counts full passes only; Repair is separately labeled. Local saving allows breaks at any point. “Learned” counts completed batches plus retained progress from the earlier prototype; “sets tested” requires the current set test. Neither claims long-term mastery.

Spanish answers normalize case, whitespace, Unicode composition, curly apostrophes and terminal sentence punctuation. Accents and ñ remain significant for credit. The current local Day 1 implementation uses explicit prompt-specific alternatives on 76 cards. See the [local content review](lemma-day1-local-preview.md) for that scope. Headword recall requests the dictionary form, with infinitives for verbs and explicitly cued forms for sentence blanks.


### Generous correction attempts — local November 1, 2026

Typed headword recall and context reviews now offer score-free retries for close spelling, accent differences and explicitly recorded forms of the expected lemma. A quiet line below the editable input asks for a correction. The answer and pronunciation remain concealed during the retry. Repeated qualifying attempts have no fixed limit. Exact accepted answers still determine when credit is awarded. Show answer and submissions outside the retry rules use the existing miss-and-repair flow.

`lemmas/answer-attempt.js` owns this interaction through `submitAnswer`, wrapping the existing exact `checkAnswer` grader. It compares against every explicitly accepted answer and their recorded forms. Spelling retries allow one insertion, deletion, substitution or adjacent transposition when both strings have at least three letters, or two edits when both have at least seven. Accent-only differences receive a specific cue even for short words. The accent comparison preserves ñ as a separate letter. A different real word can qualify for a spelling retry, so the message mentions word choice and makes no claim that the answer was correct. Semantic similarity alone does not generate a retry.

Verb retries use the singular tense inventory below. Other parts of speech use existing note forms and authored context targets. Headword prompts ask for the dictionary form on a recognized inflection. Sentence prompts ask for the form required there. For supported full-sentence and phrase answers, retries inspect the target position while requiring surrounding words to match. The same accent and edit-distance allowances also apply to these recorded forms. A likely typo in a non-headword form gets “Check the spelling and requested form. Try again.” Exact accepted answers and their spelling cues take precedence. This is an enumerated form inventory with finite spelling rules, so some plausible near attempts will still fall outside it.

The inventory uses the exact card ID and its resolved teaching note. The preview and course share the same note-resolution path. An accepted alternative contributes forms only from entries with the target card’s part of speech. This prevents forms from a same-spelling noun or pronoun from being presented as forms of a verb or adverb. Spelling proximity remains an independent route to a retry, so seres can still be flagged as close to eres without being classified as a conjugation of ser.

All 369 verb cards recognize the course’s yo, tú, and él/ella/usted forms of present indicative, imperfect indicative, preterite indicative, present perfect indicative and present subjunctive. Apply the card’s existing sense restrictions, including singular impersonal haber. Present-perfect responses retain the auxiliary and participle together. A typo in the participle can prompt a retry with the right auxiliary. A spelling shared with an eligible form qualifies. Known conjugations outside the selection use the same spelling thresholds. On an infinitive prompt, tienen is one edit from tiene and gets “Try the infinitive.” Tenemos is too far away and counts as a miss. Sentence prompts retain their context-specific cue. An explicitly required context answer always earns credit, including a plural required by the sentence. Nonverb entries retain their listed variants.

These five tenses account for about 95% of the finite indicative/subjunctive observations across all persons in the interviews. The share for the singular-only app selection is unknown. [Troya Déniz (2007), figure 1](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/2528472.pdf#page=4).

Pending retries preserve the draft, question position, first-pass score, failure count and repair queue. They save through the existing local/account progress path and survive reload. The knowledge flag and answer audio update only when grading is final. Existing saved tests remain valid. The separate Day 1 prototype shares the retry classifier and keeps its round in memory.
