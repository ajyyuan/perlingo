Summary and scope

The snapshot is accurate in most places. I found 10 clear errors, 13 likely improvements, 15 optional pedagogy items and 7 unresolved questions. None of the special-focus pairs (saber/conocer/entender/comprender, empezar/comenzar, tras/después/luego/entonces, ser/estar, por/para) teaches anything false. The serious problems are a few grading rules, mismatches between a card's meaning or example and the sense it tests, two understated negation rules, and one regional claim its source does not support.

What was audited. I read all 250 cards (meaning, example, prompt, cue, blank, accepted answers) and all 99 topics with their 175 sections (text, paired examples, sources). The snapshot holds exactly 250 cards, 99 topics and 175 sections, as stated. Only card #193 (tras) has a blank.

Grading standard. I judged each card only on its visible prompt, cue, blank and the instruction to give a headword or infinitive. An answer was flagged as unfair to reject only if it fits those visible constraints, and as wrongly accepted only if it does not.

Scope of source verification. The DLE (dle.rae.es) refused direct fetches from this workspace. I verified DLE wording only where a search engine returned the entry text, and I mark those claims "verified via indexed DLE text". The DPD (2nd edition online), NGLE, Ortografía, the RAE Libro de estilo and several cited papers were read directly or through their indexed text. Where I opened a cited page but it did not settle the claim, I say so in the Source verification log. Most of the roughly 300 DLE links that support simple meanings (for example "RAE · casa") were not individually opened. I checked those meanings against my own knowledge of the language. Treat them as reviewed, not source-verified.

How to use this report. Each finding gives the ID, the exact problem wording, the smallest correction, and evidence. Corrections that need more than a few words are meant for the explanatory overlay, so the card itself stays clean.

Tier 1 — Clear errors

These ten should be fixed before release. They are ordered by harm to the learner: wrong rejections first, then wrong rules, then misleading examples.

E1. Card #165 presidente rejects "presidenta" (grading)

Problem: the prompt is "president" and the only example is "La presidenta dirige la reunión." The accepted list is only presidente. A learner who copies the example's form is marked wrong.

Why: the DPD (2nd ed.) says the majority use has now consolidated the specific feminine presidenta. Topic word-presidente itself teaches presidenta as valid.

Smallest fix: accept presidenta, or change the example to "El presidente dirige la reunión." and keep presidenta in the overlay. Add to topic word-presidente: "presidenta is the usual form for a woman today; la presidente is also correct."

Evidence: DPD, presidente (verified).

E2. Negation after the verb is taught as optional (topics quantifiers, frequency)

Problem: quantifiers › "Amount and negative expressions" says "Negatives after a verb commonly accompany no". frequency › "Position of nunca" says nunca after a verb "commonly appears with no".

Why: this is a rule, not a tendency. A negative word after the verb needs a negative word before the verb: No vino nadie is correct, Vino nadie is not. The RAE also rejects Nadie no vino in current usage.

Smallest fix: "After the verb, nada, nadie, ninguno and nunca need a negative word before the verb, usually no: No veo nada. Before the verb, they replace no: Nada funciona." Same pattern for nunca.

Evidence: RAE, Libro de estilo: La negación (verified); NGLE §48 (opened).

E3. Card #171 pues: meaning and example teach opposite senses

Problem: meaning "so, then; in that case" and prompt "so; in that case" test the consequence sense. The only example, "Me quedo aquí, pues ha pasado casi un mes. = I stay here, since almost a month has passed", shows the causal sense. The sentence is also semantically odd (why would a month passing make someone stay?).

Why: a learner who cannot judge Spanish will conclude pues means both "since" and "so" in the same slot. Topic cause-consequence correctly separates causal pues (after what it explains) from consequence pues (set off by commas inside the clause).

Smallest fix: replace the example with one that matches the tested sense: "Todos están de acuerdo; podemos, pues, empezar. = Everyone agrees; we can therefore begin." Change the meaning to "so, therefore; also since (after the main clause)". Card #172 casi shares this example and needs its own: "Casi terminé. = I almost finished."

Evidence: DLE, pues (not opened); Miche 1994, causal pues (verified: causal pues sits between the two acts and cannot be fronted).

E4. Card #239 mostrar accepts "presentar" for "Let someone see something" (grading)

Problem: cue "Let someone see something"; accepted mostrar, presentar, enseñar.

Why: presentar means introduce a person or submit something for consideration. Topic show makes exactly this distinction. Accepting it here contradicts the topic.

Smallest fix: remove presentar. Keep mostrar, enseñar.

Evidence: DLE, presentar (not opened; standard sense).

E5. Card #53 primero rejects "primer" (grading)

Problem: the example is "El primer tren llega…" and the cue is "Position in a sequence · masculine singular". Primer is also masculine singular. Accepted: primero only.

Smallest fix: change the cue to "… masculine singular · full headword", or accept primer with a note "headword: primero". Topic order already teaches the apocope.

Evidence: DLE, primero (not opened; apocope is standard grammar).

E6. Topic location-topic › "Position in context": example does not show "above"

Problem: "La lámpara está sobre la mesa. = The lamp is above the table."

Why: the natural reading is a table lamp resting on the table. The example is meant to show the "above" sense but most readers will read "on".

Smallest fix: "La lámpara cuelga sobre la mesa. = The lamp hangs above the table."

Evidence: DLE, sobre (not opened; both senses are standard).

E7. Cards #105 bien and #106 antes share an ambiguous example

Problem: "Como bien antes de las tres. = I eat well before three."

Why: bien antes de las tres is normally read as "well before three o'clock", with bien intensifying antes. The example does not clearly show bien = "well (manner)", the sense card #105 tests.

Smallest fix: #105 "Ana escribe bien. = Ana writes well." #106 "Llego antes de las tres. = I arrive before three."

E8. Card #40 sobre: the meaning omits the tested sense

Problem: meaning "over, above, on, upon, atop"; the prompt tests "about; on the subject of" and the example shows "on the table".

Smallest fix: meaning "on; above, over; about (a topic)". Optionally, example "Un libro sobre historia. = A book about history."

E9. Card #25 hacer accepts unnatural collocations (grading)

Problem: cue "to do the homework"; accepted hacer, realizar, efectuar, ejecutar.

Why: hacer los deberes / la tarea is the natural phrase. Realizar la tarea is acceptable in formal school language. Efectuar los deberes and ejecutar la tarea are not natural for homework. Accepting them teaches a learner that they are.

Smallest fix: accept hacer, realizar. Leave the broader list on card #137 realizar, whose cue ("An action or task") supports it.

E10. Topic despues-luego › "Regional meanings": claim not supported by the cited source

Problem: "Luego can also mean promptly or right away in regions including Mexico, Chile and the Canary Islands. In El Salvador, the DLE also records quickly and early." Source: DLE, luego.

Why: the DLE marks "Prontamente, sin dilación" as desus. (disused) and "U. en Am." (used in the Americas). It names no Mexico, Chile or Canary Islands label. The El Salvador senses ("temprano", "rápidamente") are correct. Canary use is documented in the RAE's historical dictionary of Canarian Spanish, not in the DLE.

Smallest fix: "The DLE records the older sense 'promptly, without delay' as still used in the Americas, and 'early' and 'quickly' in El Salvador." If Canary use is kept, cite the Canarian historical dictionary separately.

Evidence: DLE, luego (verified via indexed DLE text); RAE historical dictionary, luego (opened).

Tier 2 — Likely improvements

Nothing here is false. Each item either risks a wrong inference, leaves out a rule the learner will need soon, or grades less precisely than the card teaches.

I2. Topic duration: "Te llamo cuando llego" invites the wrong mood

Problem: "Te llamo cuando llego. = I call you when I arrive." This is grammatical only as a habit. Card #41 correctly teaches "Llámame cuando llegues" with the subjunctive for a future arrival.

Smallest fix: "Siempre te llamo cuando llego. = I always call you when I arrive." In the overlay, add one line: "For a future event, cuando takes the subjunctive: Te llamaré cuando llegue."

I3. Topic before › "Earlier in time": antes (de) que needs its mood rule

Problem: "Before a clause, both antes de que and antes que exist." Correct, and verified in the DPD. The section does not say the verb after them is always subjunctive.

Smallest fix: add "… and the verb after them is always subjunctive: antes de que salgas."

Evidence: DPD, antes (verified for the two forms; the mood rule is standard grammar and not shown in that entry).

I4. Masculine nouns ending in -a are not marked (cards #59, #162, #245)

Problem: día, sistema and tema are masculine. Neither the cards nor topics time-nouns, method and communication-nouns say so. Problema and mano are flagged; these three are not.

Smallest fix: one overlay line each: "masculine despite -a: el día / el sistema / el tema."

I5. Topic personal-pronouns: con + mí and other exceptions

Problem: "mí follows many prepositions" is true but hides the commonest learner error, con mí.

Smallest fix: add "With con, use conmigo and contigo. After entre and según, use yo and tú." Card #191 can carry the same line in its overlay.

Evidence: DPD, pronombres personales tónicos (verified: con mí is incorrect).

I6. Card #43 también: the cue lets "además" in

Problem: cue "Ana is coming; Luis is also coming." accepts también, además. Además, viene Luis is possible but means "moreover", not the parallel "too" the card teaches. Topic addition draws exactly this line.

Smallest fix: give the cue a blank only también fits: "Ana viene. Luis ___. (= Luis too.)" Then accept only también.

I7. Card #18 como accepts "porque" in a reason-first slot

Problem: cue "… → ___ llueve, nos quedamos en casa." accepts como, porque. A fronted porque clause is possible but marked; como is the normal choice here, and topic cause-consequence teaches that.

Smallest fix: keep accepting porque (rejecting it would be harsh) but show feedback: "Correct, but como is the usual choice when the reason comes first."

I8. Headword instructions are inconsistent on adjective and determiner cards

Problem: some cards say "masculine singular headword" (#67, #175, #203). Others show a different form in the example without saying which form to type: #87 bueno (example buena), #212 pequeño (pequeña), #223 único, #90 último, #241 cualquiera (learners often type cualquier), #71 grande (gran likely).

Smallest fix: add "· masculine singular headword" to every adjective and determiner cue, or accept the feminine and apocopated forms with a headword note.

I9. Card #195 mientras: unnatural example

Problem: "Mientras trabajo mucho, bebo agua." With mucho, the clause can read as contrastive ("whereas I work a lot").

Smallest fix: "Mientras trabajo, bebo agua. = While I work, I drink water."

I10. Topic despues-luego › "Expressions to learn together"

Problem 1: "Después de todo lo que hice, se queja." is listed as an expression with its own meaning, but it is literal ("after everything I did"). The real idiom is después de todo = "after all".

Fix: "Después de todo, tenía razón. = After all, she was right."

Problem 2: "Luego luego" is shown with no region. The DLE entry carries no label, but other dictionaries mark it as Mexican and informal. Add "(esp. Mexico, informal)" in the overlay.

Problem 3: section "After something happens" says the DLE glosses luego que as "as soon as". The DLE labels that gloss Am. Add "(Americas)".

Evidence: DLE, luego (verified via indexed text).

I11. Topic degree: "Casi termino. = I'm almost finished."

Problem: in many varieties casi + present narrates a past near-miss (Casi me caigo = I almost fell). The example is ambiguous.

Smallest fix: "Ya casi termino. = I'm almost done." or "Casi he terminado."

I12. Card #50 desde: the meaning omits "from"

Problem: meaning "since (from a specified time in the past)"; example "desde aquí = from here".

Smallest fix: meaning "since; from (a starting point)". This pairs with the example swap in I1.

I13. Topic attainment › "Construction and point of view": a construction claim lacks support

Problem: "Obtener does not take that same direct-infinitive construction." The only source for this section that discusses obtener is a FundéuRAE recommendation whose title is about resultado. I could not open it.

Smallest fix: keep the claim (it matches standard usage), but cite the DLE entries for lograr and conseguir or the NGLE, or soften to "obtener is normally used with a noun".

Tier 4 — Unresolved questions

Each of these needs a source I could not reach, or a product decision.

U1. Card #18, fronted porque. How marked is Porque llueve, nos quedamos en casa in neutral speech? The NGLE chapter on causal clauses would settle it; I did not open it. The I7 recommendation (accept with feedback) holds either way.

U2. Topic source › "Naming both ends". The text approves de…a, desde…hasta and desde…a. NGLE §29.4 (verified) lists de or desde first and a, hasta or hacia second, but does not rule on mixed de…hasta. The DPD entry for de may discourage it; I did not open it. Decide whether to add a warning once checked.

U3. Card #49 muy accepts "bien"; topic quality cites the DPD for intensifying bien. Bien difícil is real and common, especially in the Americas, but it is colloquial, and I did not open the DPD bien entry to confirm the citation. Consider accepting bien with the note "muy is the neutral choice".

U4. Topic knowledge › "Where saber and conocer overlap". "Conozco la respuesta" is grammatical, but sé la respuesta is far more frequent. Without a corpus count, I suggest adding "saber is the usual choice" rather than presenting the two as equal.

U5. Card #177 partido accepts "juego". Juego for a whole match is regional (common in parts of the Americas, especially for baseball). I did not verify it in a regional dictionary. Acceptance is reasonable; the topic already explains the tennis sense.

U6. Luego de, everywhere or regional? Topic sequence says "luego de is used with regional variation" and topic despues-luego says the DLE records it in Argentina, Mexico and the Dominican Republic (verified). The DPD treats luego de que as simply correct, with no regional limit (verified). Decide whether learners should be told it is safe everywhere or mainly American.

U7. Links I could not resolve. rae.es/dpd/tilde (topics continuing-time, relative-question, accent-function), the Krohn article URL on canela.org.es, the USC page bds.usc.es/manual/perifrasis.html and the FundéuRAE page. The content claims are fine or verified elsewhere (see the log); only the links need a click-test.
