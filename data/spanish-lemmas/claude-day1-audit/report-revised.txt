Summary and scope

The snapshot is accurate in most places. After the review challenges (revision 2, below), 4 demonstrated errors remain. There are also 16 places where a clearer example or explanation would help, 15 optional pedagogy items and 8 unresolved usage questions. Two original error flags are withdrawn. None of the special-focus pairs (saber/conocer/entender/comprender, empezar/comenzar, tras/después/luego/entonces, ser/estar, por/para) teaches anything false. The remaining real problems are a grading rule that rejects a valid form, cards whose meaning or example does not match the sense they test, and under-specified answer forms.

What was audited. I read all 250 cards (meaning, example, prompt, cue, blank, accepted answers) and all 99 topics with their 175 sections (text, paired examples, sources). The snapshot holds exactly 250 cards, 99 topics and 175 sections, as stated. Only card #193 (tras) has a blank.

Grading standard. I judged each card only on its visible prompt, cue, blank and the instruction to give a headword or infinitive. An answer was flagged as unfair to reject only if it fits those visible constraints, and as wrongly accepted only if it does not.

Scope of source verification. Every finding now carries a basis label, and these are not equally strong:

Primary, read directly: I fetched the primary page itself. This applies only to DPD luego and the Krohn 2025 article.

Primary, indexed copy: I read a search engine's stored copy of the primary page. These copies can lag behind the live site. DLE and DPD copies were not stamped with an edition, and the DLE luego copy turned out to match an older edition (see E10).

User-reported primary check: you fetched the live page directly. I could not reproduce these fetches (dle.rae.es refused this workspace), so I record them as reported.

Model judgment: my own judgment of usage, not checked in a source.

A failed fetch or a missing search snippet is not evidence that a source lacks a claim. I did not open most of the roughly 300 DLE links behind simple meanings.

How to use this report. Each finding gives the ID, the exact problem wording, the smallest correction, and evidence. Corrections that need more than a few words are meant for the explanatory overlay, so the card itself stays clean.

Tier 1 — Demonstrated errors

Each of these four is shown by the snapshot itself: a valid answer the visible cue allows is rejected, or a card's own fields contradict each other. None of them depends on a usage judgment. E2, E6, E7 and E9 were moved to lower tiers, and E4 and E10 were withdrawn (see the change log).

E1. Card #165 presidente rejects "presidenta" (grading)

Problem: the prompt is "president" and the only example is "La presidenta dirige la reunión." The accepted list is only presidente. A learner who copies the example's form is marked wrong.

Why: the DPD (2nd ed.) says the majority use has now consolidated the specific feminine presidenta. Topic word-presidente itself teaches presidenta as valid.

Smallest fix: accept presidenta, or change the example to "El presidente dirige la reunión." and keep presidenta in the overlay. Add to topic word-presidente: "presidenta is the usual form for a woman today; la presidente is also correct."

Evidence: DPD, presidente (primary, indexed copy).

E3. Card #171 pues: meaning and example teach opposite senses

Problem: the meaning "so, then; in that case" and the prompt "so; in that case" test the consequence sense. The only example, "Me quedo aquí, pues ha pasado casi un mes. = I stay here, since almost a month has passed", is translated with the causal sense.

Why it is demonstrated: the card's own English gloss of its example ("since") is not among its meanings, and it is the opposite direction of reasoning from the tested sense. Topic cause-consequence separates the two uses correctly.

Smallest fix: use an example of the tested sense: "Todos están de acuerdo; podemos, pues, empezar. = Everyone agrees; we can therefore begin." Alternatively, keep the example and add "since (after the main clause)" to the meaning.

Basis: card fields (demonstrated); Miche 1994 (primary, indexed copy) for the position of causal pues.

E5. Card #53 primero rejects "primer" (grading)

Problem: the example is "El primer tren llega…" and the cue is "Position in a sequence · masculine singular". Primer is also masculine singular. Accepted: primero only.

Smallest fix: change the cue to "… masculine singular · full headword", or accept primer with a note "headword: primero". Topic order already teaches the apocope.

Evidence: DLE, primero (not opened; apocope is standard grammar).

E8. Card #40 sobre: the meaning omits the tested sense

Problem: meaning "over, above, on, upon, atop"; the prompt tests "about; on the subject of" and the example shows "on the table".

Smallest fix: meaning "on; above, over; about (a topic)". Optionally, example "Un libro sobre historia. = A book about history."

Tier 2 — Clearer teaching examples and explanations

Nothing in this tier is shown to be false. Each item is an ambiguous example, an explanation that leaves out a rule the learner will soon need, or an example that shows a different sense from the one tested. The three downgraded flags (E2, E6, E7) come first.

E2. Topics quantifiers and frequency: the negation rule is incomplete (downgraded)

Wording: quantifiers › "Amount and negative expressions": "Negatives after a verb commonly accompany no; before it, they can supply the negation themselves." frequency › "Position of nunca": "After a verb it commonly appears with no."

Issue: "commonly" does not say negation is optional, so this is not a false claim. It leaves the learner without the rule they need to build sentences: in a plain negative statement, a negative word after the verb needs a negative element before the verb. That element is usually no, but the RAE style guide also lists tampoco, nunca, ninguno and sin. Other contexts also allow these words without a preverbal negative, such as some questions and comparisons (NGLE §48.6).

Smallest correction: "In a negative statement, when nada, nadie, ninguno or nunca come after the verb, a negative word must come before it, usually no: No veo nada. Placed before the verb, they make the sentence negative themselves: Nada funciona." The same scope applies to nunca. Fuller licensing contexts can go in the overlay.

Basis: RAE, Libro de estilo: La negación (primary, indexed copy); NGLE §48.6, inductores negativos (primary, indexed copy).

E6. Topic location-topic › "Position in context": ambiguous example (downgraded)

Wording: "La lámpara está sobre la mesa. = The lamp is above the table."

Issue: the translation is a possible reading, since a hanging lamp can be sobre la mesa. A resting table lamp is also a possible reading. The section uses this sentence to show the "above" sense, so the ambiguity weakens the contrast with the "on" example just before it.

Smallest correction: "La lámpara cuelga sobre la mesa. = The lamp hangs above the table."

Basis: model judgment.

E7. Cards #105 bien and #106 antes: ambiguous shared example (downgraded)

Wording: "Como bien antes de las tres. = I eat well before three."

Issue: the Spanish, like the English, has two parses: "I eat well, before three" (manner) and "I eat well before three o'clock" (bien intensifying antes). Card #105 tests the manner sense, and this example cannot isolate it. I make no claim about which reading is more frequent.

Smallest correction: #105 "Ana escribe bien. = Ana writes well." #106 "Llego antes de las tres. = I arrive before three."

Basis: model judgment.

I2. Topic duration: "Te llamo cuando llego" invites the wrong mood

Problem: "Te llamo cuando llego. = I call you when I arrive." This is grammatical only as a habit. Card #41 correctly teaches "Llámame cuando llegues" with the subjunctive for a future arrival.

Smallest fix: "Siempre te llamo cuando llego. = I always call you when I arrive." In the overlay, add one line: "For a future event, cuando takes the subjunctive: Te llamaré cuando llegue."

I3. Topic before › "Earlier in time": antes (de) que needs its mood rule

Problem: "Before a clause, both antes de que and antes que exist." Correct. The section does not say the verb after them is subjunctive.

Smallest fix: add "… followed by the subjunctive: antes de que salgas."

Basis: DPD, antes (primary, indexed copy) for the two forms. The subjunctive rule is model judgment from standard grammar; that DPD copy does not state it.

I4. Masculine nouns ending in -a are not marked (cards #59, #162, #245)

Problem: día, sistema and tema are masculine. Neither the cards nor topics time-nouns, method and communication-nouns say so. Problema and mano are flagged; these three are not.

Smallest fix: one overlay line each: "masculine despite -a: el día / el sistema / el tema."

I5. Topic personal-pronouns: con + mí and other exceptions

Problem: "mí follows many prepositions" is true but hides the commonest learner error, con mí.

Smallest fix: add "With con, use conmigo and contigo. After entre and según, use yo and tú." Card #191 can carry the same line in its overlay.

Evidence: DPD, pronombres personales tónicos (primary, indexed copy: con mí is incorrect).

I6. Card #43 también: the cue lets "además" in

Problem: cue "Ana is coming; Luis is also coming." accepts también, además. Además, viene Luis is possible but means "moreover", not the parallel "too" the card teaches. Topic addition draws exactly this line.

Smallest fix: give the cue a blank only también fits: "Ana viene. Luis ___. (= Luis too.)" Then accept only también.

I7. Card #18 como accepts "porque" in a reason-first slot

Problem: cue "… → ___ llueve, nos quedamos en casa." accepts como, porque. The card and topic cause-consequence teach como for a reason placed first, but the grader gives no signal of that.

Smallest fix: keep accepting porque, and show feedback: "Accepted. Como is the form this lesson teaches when the reason comes first." How natural fronted porque is remains open (U1).

I8. Headword instructions are inconsistent on adjective and determiner cards

Problem: some cards say "masculine singular headword" (#67, #175, #203). Others show a different form in the example without saying which form to type: #87 bueno (example buena), #212 pequeño (pequeña), #223 único, #90 último, #241 cualquiera (learners often type cualquier), #71 grande (gran likely).

Smallest fix: add "· masculine singular headword" to every adjective and determiner cue, or accept the feminine and apocopated forms with a headword note.

I9. Card #195 mientras: unnatural example

Problem: "Mientras trabajo mucho, bebo agua." Mientras can also introduce a contrast, and with mucho the sentence is less clearly about two things at the same time.

Smallest fix: "Mientras trabajo, bebo agua. = While I work, I drink water."

Basis: model judgment.

I10. Topic despues-luego › "Expressions to learn together"

Point 1 (remains): "Después de todo lo que hice, se queja." is listed among expressions with meanings of their own, but it reads literally ("after everything I did"). The idiom después de todo = "after all" is better shown as "Después de todo, tenía razón. = After all, she was right." Basis: model judgment.

Point 2 (revised): luego luego is shown without a region. Larousse's bilingual dictionary (a secondary source) marks it as Mexican and informal. I no longer cite the DLE here. Optional overlay: "(esp. Mexico, informal)" once the current DLE label is confirmed.

Point 3 (withdrawn): I said the DLE labels luego que "Am.". That came from the same older indexed copy as E10. Your direct fetch shows no Americas label. No change needed.

I11. Topic degree: "Casi termino. = I'm almost finished."

Problem: casi + present can also narrate a past near-miss (Casi me caigo = I almost fell), so "I'm almost finished" is one of two readings.

Smallest fix: "Ya casi termino. = I'm almost done." or "Casi he terminado."

Basis: model judgment.

I12. Card #50 desde: the meaning omits "from"

Problem: meaning "since (from a specified time in the past)"; example "desde aquí = from here".

Smallest fix: meaning "since; from (a starting point)". This pairs with the example swap in I1.

I13. Topic attainment › "Construction and point of view": a construction claim lacks support

Problem: "Obtener does not take that same direct-infinitive construction." The only source for this section that discusses obtener is a FundéuRAE recommendation whose title is about resultado. I could not open it.

Smallest fix: keep the claim (it matches standard usage), but cite the DLE entries for lograr and conseguir or the NGLE, or soften to "obtener is normally used with a noun".

Tier 4 — Unresolved usage judgments

Each of these turns on a usage judgment that no primary source I reached settles, or on a product decision. None meets the threshold for rejecting an answer or calling the snapshot wrong.

E9. Card #25 hacer: style of accepted collocations (downgraded)

Wording: cue "to do the homework"; accepted hacer, realizar, efectuar, ejecutar.

Issue: hacer los deberes / la tarea is the everyday phrase. Realizar, efectuar and ejecutar with tarea are more formal or administrative. I have no primary evidence that they are invalid, so excluding them would rest on style alone.

Options: keep all four; or, if the card should teach the everyday verb, constrain the cue ("everyday verb: to do the homework") and then accept hacer. Either is defensible.

Basis: model judgment.

U1. Card #18, fronted porque. How natural is Porque llueve, nos quedamos en casa in neutral speech? The NGLE chapter on causal clauses would settle it; I did not open it. The I7 recommendation (accept, with feedback) holds either way.

U2. Topic source › "Naming both ends". The text approves de…a, desde…hasta and desde…a. NGLE §29.4 (primary, indexed copy) lists those pairs but does not rule on mixed de…hasta. Check the DPD de entry before adding any warning.

U3. Card #49 muy accepts "bien"; topic quality cites the DPD for intensifying bien. Bien difícil is real but colloquial. I did not open the DPD bien entry. Consider feedback such as "muy is the neutral choice".

U4. Topic knowledge › "Where saber and conocer overlap". "Conozco la respuesta" is grammatical. Whether it deserves equal billing with sé la respuesta needs a corpus count; I have none.

U5. Card #177 partido accepts "juego". Juego for a whole match is regional. I did not verify it in a regional dictionary. Acceptance is reasonable.

U6 (revised). Luego de, everywhere or regional? Topic despues-luego says the DLE records luego de in Argentina, Mexico and the Dominican Republic. My confirmation came from the same older indexed DLE copy that misled E10, so I no longer count it as verified against the 24th edition. The DPD treats luego de que as correct with no regional limit (primary, read directly). Re-check the live DLE entry, then decide whether learners should be told it is safe everywhere.

U7. Pages I could not open. All dle.rae.es pages (fetches refused), rae.es/dpd/tilde (topics continuing-time, relative-question, accent-function), the Krohn URL on canela.org.es, the USC page bds.usc.es/manual/perifrasis.html and the FundéuRAE page.
